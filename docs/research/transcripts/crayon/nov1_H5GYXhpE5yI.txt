Wasn't this just the company that made Claude? How did it become the company leading the AI revolution? Well, it all started with a kid named Dario. 1980s San Francisco. Dario Amodi grows up with two parents who take their work seriously. His father is an Italian leather craftsman. His mother manages library construction projects. In their house, having a moral compass matters more than anything else. Hey nerd, give me your lunch money. Leave him alone. Why do you care? You're not even his friend? I am now. He's also very good at science, like annoyingly good. He studied physics at Caltech, then Stanford, and at just 23 began a PhD in theoretical physics at Princeton. His plan is to study the biggest mysteries in existence, then his father dies from hepatitis C. And suddenly, the universe feels much less important. Forget black holes. Why couldn't science save him? So he walks out of physics, into biophysics, computational neuroscience, studying the wiring inside the brain, eventually hunting for cancer signals, hiding in protein data. Then comes the part that actually breaks him. A few years after his father's death, a new treatment pushes the disease cure rate from 50% to 95%. A few years earlier, and his father would still be alive. That thought never leaves him. Maybe science doesn't lack answers. Maybe we're just too slow at finding them. So he begins looking for something that can search faster than any human, introducing artificial intelligence. After working at Baidu and Google, Dario joins OpenAI in 2016 and eventually leads its AI safety team. The goal? Built a machine that understands language, not one built from rules, one that learns. So they build something they call GPT, generative pre-trained transformer. So what do we teach it? Nothing. Feed it a mountain of books and articles and make it guess the next word.

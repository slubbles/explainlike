Take a look at this simple simulation where this humanoid tries its best to stand up, and currently it has a really difficult time doing the most basic tasks like standing up. Now, we could use RL to reward the humanoid to actually stand up, but even that is not really a trivial task. What you're seeing here is two different outcomes depending on the RL algorithm that was used, where one stands up a little bit and then falls and repeats the cycle, while the other one just freezes without fully standing up. This is one of the common problems that occurs in RL called exploration versus exploitation. Taking different actions like this might lead to a better strategy in the end, where clearly in this example leads to the humanoid actually standing up but eventually falls over and over again. Whereas exploitation is something like this where the humanoid just tries to maximize what it already learned without taking big risks and exploring for potentially a better solution like standing up. And the cost that's involved here is that exploring can end up wasting a lot of compute on actions that doesn't really go anywhere, while too much exploitation can also lead to this mediocre strategy where it doesn't really go beyond sub-optimal strategy like this, where it just settles there thinking that that's the best it can do. Now when you extrapolate this simple analogy like this humanoid into much more complex environments like Super Mario, robotic simulation, gaming and more, you start to realize that the challenge is really how to balance between the two.
